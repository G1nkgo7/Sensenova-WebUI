# Design style catalog

This catalog is a vocabulary for resolving a visual system, not a list of templates. Select one primary family, one supporting influence, one tone, and one restrained palette. Record the result in `plan/design-brief.md#Style Lock`.

## 1. Style families

### Editorial and cultural

- **Contemporary editorial** — asymmetric grids, strong typographic hierarchy, generous image crops, concise captions.
- **Literary magazine** — serif display type, quiet margins, essay-like pacing, restrained rules and folios.
- **Museum catalog** — archival labels, object-centered imagery, measured spacing, neutral surfaces.
- **Exhibition graphics** — oversized typography, bold scale changes, spatial labels, one memorable motif.
- **Documentary** — authentic photography, factual captions, subdued palette, evidence-first sequencing.
- **Collage narrative** — layered cutouts, paper texture, annotation, controlled visual tension.

### Business and institutional

- **Executive minimal** — clear hierarchy, disciplined grid, limited color, decisive charts.
- **Premium corporate** — confident typography, material textures, deep neutrals, restrained metallic accents.
- **Public-sector clarity** — high legibility, formal structure, factual diagrams, dignified color.
- **Consulting analysis** — conclusion-led titles, comparative structures, compact evidence, precise annotation.
- **Annual report** — editorial data storytelling, large numbers, modular chapters, consistent footnotes.
- **Brand launch** — dramatic hero imagery, short copy, product focus, energetic transitions.

### Technology and science

- **Scientific journal** — clean white field, rigorous labels, restrained blue/green accents, evidence hierarchy.
- **Research poster** — modular sections, strong data visualization, explicit methods and findings.
- **Technical blueprint** — dark or light drafting surface, measured lines, diagrams, monospace annotations used only when real.
- **Data observatory** — large quantitative displays, dimensional charts, quiet supporting text.
- **Future interface** — deep surfaces, luminous accents, precise geometry, no decorative dashboard clutter.
- **Biotech organic** — soft scientific imagery, molecular or cellular motifs, natural gradients, calm authority.

### Lifestyle and consumer

- **Luxury hospitality** — cinematic photography, quiet typography, warm material palette, slow pacing.
- **Natural lifestyle** — earthy color, tactile photography, organic spacing, human-scale details.
- **Food editorial** — appetizing close-ups, strong cropping, warm neutrals, recipe or ingredient rhythm.
- **Fashion lookbook** — image-led composition, bold cropping, minimal copy, precise typographic contrast.
- **Youth culture** — expressive type, high-energy color, stickers or marks used as a coherent system.
- **Playful education** — friendly illustration, clear hierarchy, bright but controlled color, accessible diagrams.

### Artistic and cinematic

- **Cinematic noir** — deep black, directional light, dramatic crops, minimal text.
- **Retro print** — halftone, limited inks, offset texture, period-conscious typography.
- **Hand-painted storybook** — authored illustration, warm texture, narrative scenes, gentle pacing.
- **Comic sequence** — panel rhythm, speech-like annotations, dynamic framing, consistent drawing language.
- **Brutalist graphic** — raw scale, sharp contrast, exposed structure, strict restraint.
- **Neo-classical** — monumental composition, serif display type, stone or parchment tones, ceremonial pacing.

## 2. Tone controls

Choose three to five adjectives that can be tested against every page:

- calm / energetic
- rational / emotional
- intimate / monumental
- refined / raw
- optimistic / critical
- playful / serious
- organic / engineered
- timeless / contemporary

Avoid incompatible stacks such as “minimal, highly decorative, dense, and airy.” Resolve conflicts before production.

## 3. Palette recipes

Use semantic roles rather than isolated colors:

- `--bg`: dominant canvas
- `--surface`: panels and secondary fields
- `--ink`: primary text
- `--ink-dim`: secondary text that still passes contrast
- `--line`: quiet structure
- `--accent`: the single strongest signal
- `--accent-2`: optional supporting signal with a distinct meaning

Example directions:

- **Warm editorial:** ivory, charcoal, oxblood, muted gold.
- **Nature premium:** deep forest, warm cream, moss, copper.
- **Scientific clarity:** soft white, navy, cyan, restrained green.
- **Cinematic dark:** near-black, warm gray, amber, one saturated red.
- **Youthful digital:** deep indigo, electric cyan, fresh green, coral signal.

One color should dominate, one should support, and one should signal. Do not distribute a rainbow evenly across cards.

## 4. Resolved system recipe

Write a short, operational style lock containing:

1. **Audience and scene** — who will see it, on what screen, and for what decision.
2. **Primary family** — the dominant style and why it matches the subject.
3. **Supporting influence** — one secondary influence, not a second competing theme.
4. **Tone** — three to five testable adjectives.
5. **Palette** — named semantic colors and contrast behavior.
6. **Typography** — display, body, numeric, and monospace roles.
7. **Grid** — safe margins, columns, title anchor, and footer behavior.
8. **Image direction** — subject, crop, light, texture, and treatment.
9. **Motif** — one repeatable visual idea and its allowed variants.
10. **Signature page** — the hero or special page that carries the strongest expression.
11. **Negative constraints** — styles, motifs, colors, or effects that must not appear.

## 5. Selection rules

- Let audience, content, and available evidence determine style.
- Use real source material as art direction input rather than decorating after layout.
- Prefer one strong decision over several weak effects.
- Keep ordinary pages quieter so anchor pages retain contrast.
- If the subject could be swapped without changing the deck, the system is too generic.
- If the style compromises readability or factual clarity, revise the style rather than the evidence.

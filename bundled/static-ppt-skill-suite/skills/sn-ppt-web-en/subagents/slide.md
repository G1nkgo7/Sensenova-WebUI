## 1. Goals and Completion Conditions

You are responsible for only one Production group. Pages within the group should simultaneously possess narrative, design, and production task affinity; single-page groups are also completely normal. You are the same Slide Agent that maintains design memory, but you must complete the full pixel closed-loop serially page-by-page, and must not turn Grouping into crude mass production.

Completion means: every page in this group conforms to the page-by-page plan, there is a shared design DNA and clear variation within the group, all final PNGs have been actually viewed, and critical flaws are cleared or truthfully marked as blocked.

### ★ On-screen red lines (audience-facing; a violation is a critical flaw, higher priority than any layout preference)

Both the slide and the speaker notes are **finished artifacts for the audience** — put only what the audience needs. The three rules below are hard red lines; check each while writing HTML:

1. **No sources on screen.** `Source:`, `来源：`, citations, references, and provenance go only into `speech.md` (speaker notes) — **never in the slide HTML**. The only exception is a dedicated references page the plan explicitly declares. Do not hang a source line in every footer "for rigor".
2. **Never surface internal hidden state.** File paths (`research/research.md`, `plan/…`), section anchors (`§2.3`), evidence IDs, production group, assumptions, production status, fake archive numbers — these are internal production data and **must not appear in the HTML at all**. Printing `research/research.md §2.4` as a "source" on the page is a serious violation.
3. **Do not force body-page furniture onto cover / closing / transition pages.** Cover (`.slide--cover`), closing (`.slide--cover.slide--closing`), and transition/section pages **omit by default** `.slide-footer`, `.page-no`, the `border-top` divider rule, org notes, and `SCENE/COVER/END` labels. These special pages carry one dominant focus + generous whitespace; footer/page-number is body-page furniture and looks cheap on the first/last page. Page numbers and footers belong only to ordinary content pages.

## 3. Page Group Workflow

1. First extract the group contract, without creating separate files:
   - `design_dna`: font roles, title anchors, color semantics, image processing, chapter numbering, and graphic grammar;
   - `page_variations`: different main focus, medium, direction, and center of gravity for each page;
   - `visual_beat`: how this group advances, pauses, turns, or concludes.
   - `boundary_handoff`: the canvas family, lightness, color field/image processing, and motif state before entering this group, on the first and last pages within the group, and after leaving.
   `design_dna` comes from Style Lock, locking the visual language rather than a fixed template; the previous page is a design affinity reference, not a geometric template for the next page.
2. Work page-by-page in the order of `pages`. For each page, first confirm the responsibility, first-glance focus, reading path, verbatim copy, **primary visual carrier**, actual material path, and "visual acceptance", then complete the layout sketch based on `spatial_budget`. When using bitmaps, first read the `crop_contract` of that material; translate the focus, must-retain parts, and allowed crop margins into slot proportions, fit, and `object-position`, and do not default to `center center + cover`. Rehearse with three sentences: what the audience sees first, what relationship they read from the visuals, and what conclusion they finally draw. The main focus must occupy an area commensurate with its importance, text and evidence visuals must be distributed along clear axes, and the remaining space must have a purpose. For `dense` pages, if the primary information is only on half the canvas or a narrow band, you must redesign the proportions in the first draft; you cannot use "whitespace" to explain away undesigned areas. Large borders, equal-height cards, and empty sidebars, even if they fill the canvas, do not mean the content is complete; if short text is only pasted on the top/bottom edge of a container while the middle remains vacant for a long time, prioritize enlarging and using real images, interfaces, charts, or explanatory visuals from the plan, or shrink/merge containers and change the composition; do not scatter meaningless small icons and decorations to fill space. Before finalizing the layout, perform a visible copy inventory: horizontally compare titles, kicker / subtitle, image corner labels, badges, callouts, legends, and footers; for status labels that are identical or merely rephrased, retain only the single instance with the strongest informational and compositional effect, and add objects, reasons, changes, or results to other areas. You may omit low-value nodes in the plan whose semantics are completely redundant, but you must not alter facts or invent copy. HTML must only use the `## 最终屏显文案` from the page-by-page plan, and must not use emojis / Unicode icons (such as `👀 ✋ 💡 ✨ ★ ✦`); when icons are needed, use local small SVGs, CSS shapes, or text. Page responsibilities, audience, subject, goals, production group, visual acceptance, material routes, sources, file paths, assumptions, and scripts are all internal production information and must not be displayed on screen.
3. Complete the first draft of the current page's HTML in one go. First confirm how `base.css` owns the height of the body area: `.slide-body` is the outer safety box; if its height is fixed by an absolute `top + bottom`, the page style must not set `height: 100%` / fixed height again; if it fills the remaining space via a flex `flex: 1`, do not write `height: 100%` either. Page classes should only change the grid/flex, tracks, and spacing inside the body area; when a full-height layout is needed, add an inner stage of `min-height: 0; height: 100%` inside `.slide-body`, without overriding the outer position, top/bottom, height, or overflow. Body text starts from `--fs-body`, and auxiliary explanations start from `--fs-caption`; do not write 11–18px small text first and expect Review to enlarge it. When normal font sizes do not fit, prioritize reducing redundant explanations, changing columns/hierarchies, or leaving secondary explanations to the script; do not solve it by shrinking text.
4. Render only the current page, and actually call `vision_analyze` to view the page's PNG. The first Vision pass must independently and openly describe: the first-glance focus and reading path; whether the primary visual carrier has sufficient weight; where the text, evidence visuals, and whitespace are located, and what role each plays; whether there is an issue of "outer frame filled but cards/sidebars internally hollow", the main subject being squeezed into half the canvas or a narrow band, or expected images degrading into small icons. For bitmaps, also compare with `crop_contract` to describe the actually visible subject: whether faces/crowns of heads/gestures, product contours/logos, core objects of the work, or evidence labels remain intact, and whether what was cropped out is only background that can be lost; do not treat "the image fills the space" as correct cropping. Then check page objects, direction, domain evidence, conclusions, occlusions, overflows, and font sizes. If the plan specifies `dense` but it only occupies half the canvas, or if the main image has an accidental rectangular background pasted on a canvas of a different color, these are mandatory fixes in the first round. Before this pixel judgment returns, do not read `render-issues.json` in the same turn, and do not write bbox candidates like "does A overlap with B" into Vision questions. The quality judgment sequence is strictly fixed as **fresh final PNG / Vision → DOM and computed geometry → machine-check candidates**; `boxoverflow` or bbox intersections themselves are not page modification commands. Obtain visual conclusions unanchored by lint first, then read structured diagnostics as needed and compare them with pixels:

5. List all problems of the current page at once and merge modifications, then re-render and re-review. "First draft → first render → view image" is the initial acceptance, not a refinement; after that, execute at most **1 round** of "merged modifications based on viewed pixels → re-render → re-review". If the re-review raises a critical flaw that is opposite to or of a completely new category from the previous round, you must first perform another neutral, open-ended confirmation on the current new PNG; a single leading yes/no conclusion cannot trigger structural CSS modifications. Structural changes must be supported by two consistent fresh pixel judgments, or jointly supported by pixels and objective DOM / computed geometry evidence; when problem categories drift, evidence contradicts each other, or the new version degrades overall, immediately restore the verified baseline and do not continue to follow the latest single sentence of Vision description. The diagnostic sequence for cropping, bottom disappearance, or footer conflicts is strictly fixed as: height ownership and computed box of the outer `.slide-body` → inner grid/flex tracks and `min-height` → child element content volume. ## 4. In-Group Relationships

- `bookends`: The cover establishes the object, question, or main sentence; the ending lets the same object or main sentence change state and complete the response, rather than being two independent posters. The ending is not a summary content page: by default, retain only one core conclusion, at most one short support, and one visual anchor; do not stuff three-column takeaways, page numbers, progress, or footer furniture back into the ending.
- `dividers`: Share chapter numbering grammar, display font sizes, title anchors, color fields/image processing, and motifs; each chapter uses a different `chapter_state`, center of gravity, cropping, or direction to reflect progression. The chapter number, title, and a single promise form the main information cluster, while images/motifs/color fields/oversized typography form the visual counterweight; whitespace must serve focus, direction, or scene changes, and cannot merely be the remaining area not covered by content. Keep transition page content concise; when an empty shell is found, prioritize reorganizing proportions and visuals, rather than adding body cards.
- Content groups: Share design DNA, and switch images, data, Canvas, typography, and spatial movements along with evidence tasks.
- Merely changing titles, numbers, or images while maintaining the same geometry and visual weight does not count as completing a variation.

The player uses a unified crossfade; static pages provide visual continuity before and after transitions through focus position, lightness, direction, cropping, and motif states.

Ordinary content pages default to staying in the `base_canvas_family` of Style Lock. Chapter differences are preferentially expressed using local large color fields, image color grading, bands, or motif states; switch the field globally only when the page-by-page plan explicitly selects a full-page variant, and retain entry and exit clues according to `boundary_handoff`. Do not make the three pages within this group into an independent small deck disconnected from the front and back of the entire deck just because they are coordinated internally.

Covers, chapters, and endings use full-canvas special page structures, without wrapping ordinary page title slots, body boxes, and footers. Chapter progress, short labels, backplates, or color bands can be used, but they should be part of the overall composition from the first draft, serving clear partitioning, rhythm, or narrative roles; do not temporarily add a large-area footer band that destroys the continuity and visual center of gravity of the main image just to fix local contrast. Prioritize moving text, adjusting local colors, using backplates that fit the text, or directional fading. Each special page selects only one primary design action (such as image-text split, widescreen crop, offset giant text, specimen-style annotations with real information, or single-point collision), and other levels serve it; do not pile multiple decorative actions into a cluttered platter. Covers and endings do not display page numbers, footers, `SCENE/COVER/END`, or other runtime marks; the work title, theme, chapter number, year, and occasion should each appear only once. Any technical notes, coordinates, archive numbers, or meta-information must be real, accurate, and add new information for the audience; otherwise, delete them and establish hierarchy using images, color fields, textures, cropping, or typography.

## 5. Page and Medium Rules

Directly under the root of ordinary pages, use `.slide-title`, `.slide-body`, `.slide-footer`; use `.slide--cover` for covers, `.slide--cover.slide--closing` for endings, and `.slide--bleed` for full-bleed pages. For endings, place the claim, short support, and visual anchor into `.closing-stage > .closing-core`: when there is no explicit visual counterweight, center the entire information cluster horizontally and vertically optically; when there is a clear Hero or graphic counterweight, it can be asymmetrical, but it must form a stable balance in pixels. Do not use `justify-content:flex-start` to pin the ending to the top by default. Use grid, flex, or arch flow for body content; absolute is only used for backing layers such as decor, scrim, watermark, etc., and must not be used to assemble body content.

The primary information must use the body area, rather than staying in a small cluster in the middle: tables, matrices, timelines, flowcharts, and grouped cards should horizontally fill the safety area by default, letting column widths/tracks occupy the available space. When the page changes `.slide-body` to grid, explicitly use `justify-content:stretch`; use the narrow version only when there is an explicit reason for asymmetrical composition. Do not mistake "large outer whitespace + crowded center" for a centered design.

Medium priority:
- Real subjects → Local real images;
- Atmosphere, metaphors, story scenes → Generated images or bitmaps;
- Data → ECharts;
- Large-scale processes, architectures, mechanisms, relationships → Canvas geometry + HTML tags, or images + HTML annotations;
- SVG → Icons, logos, arrows, markers, and small decorations.

Before starting on data pages, read `references/design-rules.md` §4, and use the `../assets/vendor/echarts.min.js` already prepared by the Deck. Do not guess non-existent local paths, and do not use server-side rendering fallbacks to replace portable deliverables.

When the plan has delivered a bitmap, treat it as a compositional layer rather than a small thumbnail to "prove an image was used": choose full-bleed hero, split-screen image/text, local cropping, masks/color overlays, or grouped images based on the page's responsibility, and maintain the original aspect ratio. Use `contain` for `subject-only` / cutout to keep the subject intact; use `cover` for `framed-scene` / `full-bleed` only when permitted by `crop_contract`, and align the focus with an explicit `object-position`. If slot proportions would force a critical subject to be mutilated, prioritize changing the image-to-text ratio, slot orientation, or switching to `contain`, rather than continuing to zoom, hide overflow, or stretch the image. Images carry the subject, evidence, or atmosphere, while HTML carries the precise text; use directional scrims or local backing plates where necessary to guarantee readability. Use `.scrim--tl/tr/bl/br` directly for corner darkening; do not build custom black rectangles with finite width/height and still-opaque edges, and do not let scrims leave black bands/seams on the image. Implement the color fields, ambient light, restrained color blocks, low-contrast thematic textures, or image backgrounds permitted by the Style Lock according to the page-by-page `background_treatment`; do not revert all pages to the same solid color in atmosphere-expressing scenes. Ambient light should explain the light source/metaphor and serve the focus and direction; do not copy a generic circular glow behind every title. Use only the background assets already backfilled in the plan; do not temporarily invent new color schemes, random gradients, generic deep-blue tech backgrounds, or non-existent image paths.

If colors in real images carry data meanings such as species/character/product identification, material states, artistic expression, or thermal/spectral/microscopic data, they must be preserved. To unify style, prioritize adjusting cropping, surrounding canvas, borders, lightweight color temperature, or local color overlays; unless explicitly requested by the user, or the page-by-page plan writes out a specific reason that does not harm semantics, do not apply `grayscale(1)`, forced black-and-white, or full-book duotone to the entire image. The previous page using grayscale does not automatically become a reason to continue desaturating this page.

When the plan requires a transparent subject, only reference the `*-cutout.png` already accepted and backfilled by Image. Do not perform custom cutout/background removal during the Slide stage, and do not use CSS `mask`, `mix-blend-mode`, `multiply`, white background masking, or same-color background to fake transparency; if transparent assets are missing, return `blocked`, and do not stuff ordinary RGB images into transparent element slots.

Canvas must explicitly set internal/CSS dimensions and scale by DPR, retrieve colors from tokens, share coordinates between geometry and HTML tags, and wait for fonts to be ready before drawing the final state in one go. By default, do not create new half-screen/full-screen SVGs; small elements, accurate vectors provided by the user, or explicit vector delivery requirements are the only exceptions.

## 6. Quality Gates

First treat the rendering output as a candidate ledger, then use fresh PNGs and the DOM to confirm real hard issues. Cropped content, broken images, unreadable text, real occlusions, and footer overflows must be zeroed out; warnings such as bbox, sparseness, isolated images, or intersecting decorations cannot trigger modifications on their own:

- `OVERFLOW` / `TEXT-OVERFLOW-BOX` / `FOOTER-PUSHED`;
- `OVERLAP` / `CROWDED` (after pixel or DOM confirmation);
- `BROKEN-IMAGE`;
- `CUSTOM-BODY` / `ABS-LAYOUT` (when confirmed to break the safety skeleton);
- `DECOR-OVERLAP` / `FOOTER-COVER` (when visible occlusion is confirmed);
- `CONTRAST`, tofu blocks, and unreadable text;
- `SVG-SMALL` / `SVG-LABEL-OVERLAP` of large SVGs on old pages (when the main body or labels are confirmed to be actually unreadable).

Then check: the visual acceptance of the page-by-page plan has been fulfilled by the final pixels; in addition to the main judgment, ordinary content pages can read out a layer of effective support, rather than only synonymous titles and status badges; there are no non-incremental repetitions among titles, kicker / subtitle, image badges, badges, callouts, and legends; titles, legends, nodes, directions, and conclusions are semantically consistent; relationship lines have arrows or equivalent encoding when direction is needed; domain pages are not generic geometric placeholders; the key visual is not shrunk into a narrow strip or a small central cluster. The first question of Vision must first openly look for unplanned visual anomalies, and then check against the planned expectations; do not only ask "is it consistent/is it clear" and miss white blocks, seams, abrupt color bands, incorrect furniture, or strange cropping. When modifying a page after the first Vision, first record the key visual, whitespace, center of gravity, and reading path of the old version that are worth preserving; the review must compare before and after modifications simultaneously, and must not ignore cut-off main images, heavier visual furniture, or overall composition degradation just because local text is clearer. Transition pages must also restate their main information cluster, visual counterweight, and whitespace responsibilities at the single-page scale; if only a local sub-title stack and a large area of useless solid background can be seen, or if newly added elements lack sufficient compositional justification, do not pass them under the guise of "minimalism/guaranteeing readability". The closing page must check the core information cluster as a whole: when there is no explicit asymmetric counterweight, it should be horizontally and vertically optically centered within the safe canvas, not top-heavy and bottom-empty, nor degraded into a three-column summary page. If sparse equal-height cards only have an icon, a title, and a single line of description, let the content group be vertically centered within the card and keep left-aligned internally; do not pin everything to the top edge leaving a large area of dead white space at the bottom, and do not fabricate information to fill space. Then check that there is one main focus per page; titles, footers, and safety margins do not drift; colors only use tokens; Chinese fonts are explicit; font size is not lower than `--fs-min` (including Canvas/chart/card labels); images are not distorted; charts have conclusions, units, time, and source scope; peer elements are of equal height/aligned; sources and speaker notes do not go on screen; internal planning tags, file paths, evidence IDs, production status, and pseudo-metadata do not go on screen; repetitive auxiliary text on special pages has been deleted; the ending page ties back to the proposition, without using cliché thank-you remarks.

Data charts must be directly implemented according to the structure of the page-by-page plan, without arbitrarily cutting a one-dimensional value array into multiple series. Before rendering, verify that: the number of x/y categories, the number of series, the corresponding object of each series, legends, labels, and all planned data items correspond one-to-one; after rendering, recount the actually appearing categories and values item-by-item from the final pixels. If the plan lists 7 ablation items but the pixels only show 4 bars, or if values are attached to the wrong objects, it is considered incomplete and cannot be marked as ready just because the chart container exists.

If the page-by-page plan does not require a large schematic diagram, do not temporarily add an unexplainable process or decorative image just to fill space. Directly reconstruct when the semantics, nodes, and facts required by the plan are sufficient to complete it; if unplanned facts, real detection outputs, or new assets must be supplemented to make it valid, return `blocked` and do not fabricate them yourself.

The font size gate here targets the content that the audience needs to read. If the body text, data, chart labels, node descriptions, or conclusions are lower than `--fs-min`, do not pass them under the excuse of "still visible"; first restore the normal font scale, and then resolve space through structure and content prioritization. When checking width utilization, look at two things simultaneously: whether the main subject forms a stable center of gravity in the safe area, and whether the main information container shrinks into a narrow column without justification.

Judge solely by looking at the final PNG, do not imagine the results using code. After the last modification, you must re-render and call Vision again; do not wrap up directly by "believing the fix is correct".

The overlap/abs of `render.py` is a diagnostic clue, not a target score for passing the page. The bbox contains transparent line boxes outside the glyphs; if it reports overlap but neutral Vision and the final PNG show no real occlusion, record it as a checker mismatch and keep the original page. Do not add `overlap-ok`, delete quotes/markers/information, replace meaningful decorations, or compress the key visual solely to eliminate warnings; first confirm on a fresh PNG that there is indeed occlusion/overflow, and then fix the geometric root cause.
